# Core AI tells (general, cross-platform)

These show up in every format. Fix them on top of the platform-specific work in `platform-tells.md`. Organized fastest-to-spot first.

## Conversation leftovers (delete on sight)

The dead giveaways that no human edited the text. One pass to remove all of these instantly de-AIs a piece.

- "Great question," "Certainly," "Of course," "You're absolutely right"
- "I hope this helps," "Let me know if you'd like me to expand," "Feel free to reach out"
- "As of my last update," "based on the information available," "while details are limited"
- "Let's dive in," "let's explore," "let's break this down," "without further ado"
- "In conclusion," "in summary," "to wrap up" (just end)

## Word-level

- **AI vocab cluster.** delve, leverage, tapestry, testament, robust, landscape (figurative), realm, foster, cultivate, pivotal, crucial, vital, seamless, vibrant, intricate, nuanced, harness, elevate, unlock, empower, navigate (figurative), embark. One by accident is fine; a cluster is a tell. Swap for plain words: leverage → use, foster → build, delve into → look at, utilize → use.
- **Copula avoidance.** "serves as / stands as / acts as / boasts / represents" where "is / has" works. "The tool serves as a solution" → "The tool is a solution," or better, say what it does.
- **Em dashes.** Heavy em dash use mimics punchy sales copy. Default to commas, periods, or parentheses. Avoid em dashes entirely.
- **Curly quotes and perfect ellipses** (" " …) pasted from a chatbot. Use straight quotes and three periods.
- **Transition-word crutch.** "Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally / Consequently / Notably" opening sentences, especially several in a row. Delete most; the logic survives.

## Sentence-level

- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not just X, it's Y." "It's less about X and more about Y." "This isn't about X. It's about Y." The most-cited tell of the era. State the thing directly instead.
- **Tailing negations.** Clipped negative tacked on the end: "...no guessing," "...no fluff," "...no wasted motion." Fold into a real clause.
- **Rule of three, forced.** Everything in triples ("fast, simple, and powerful"). The third item is usually padding. Cut to two when two is true, or one.
- **False ranges.** "from the boardroom to the breakroom," "from startups to enterprises" where the two ends aren't a real scale. Just name what you mean.
- **Superficial -ing tails.** Sentences ending in "highlighting / underscoring / reflecting / showcasing / ensuring / demonstrating..." that add fake depth. Delete the tail or make it a real sentence.
- **Synonym cycling.** Refusing to repeat a noun, swapping to "the individual / this entity / the central figure." Repeat the plain word.
- **Flat rhythm.** Every sentence the same medium length. The deepest tell. Break it: short jab, then a longer run, then short again. Read aloud to catch the metronome.

## Substance-level

- **Significance inflation.** "marks a pivotal moment," "a testament to," "in the ever-evolving landscape of," "plays a crucial role," "leaves a lasting mark." Cut the editorializing about how momentous something is; state the fact.
- **Promotional / brochure language.** "nestled in the heart of," "breathtaking," "stunning," "must-have," "game-changing," "world-class," "boasts." Facts replace adjectives.
- **Vague attribution / ghost citations.** "studies show," "experts agree," "research suggests," "it's widely known" with no named source. Name the real source or cut the claim. Never invent one.
- **Excessive hedging.** "could potentially possibly," "it might be argued that," stacks of qualifiers. One clean hedge max.
- **Filler phrases.** "in order to" → "to"; "due to the fact that" → "because"; "at this point in time" → "now"; "it is important to note that" → (cut); "has the ability to" → "can"; "in today's fast-paced world" → (delete); "when it comes to" → (usually deletable).
- **Persuasive-authority tropes.** "at its core," "the real question is," "what truly matters," "make no mistake," "let's be honest," "the heart of the matter." Windups that pretend to reveal deep truth. Delete and say the point.
- **Generic positive conclusions.** "the future looks bright," "exciting times ahead," "a step in the right direction," "only time will tell," "the possibilities are endless." End on something concrete, or just stop.
- **Personified callback.** Giving objects memory or agency for fake resonance. Cut it or replace with a real concrete image.

## Formatting (for written posts, not scripts)

- **Bold-label-colon bullets.** "**Speed:** It's faster." Circular and mechanical. Write as prose or use plain bullets.
- **Over-bolding.** Several bold phrases per paragraph. Strip almost all.
- **Title Case Headings.** Use sentence case.
- **Emoji as a structural system.** One per bullet, same family. Keep at most a single human touch.
- **Fragmented headers.** A heading followed by a one-line restatement of the heading before the real content. Delete the restatement.

## The vibe tests (the real ones)

When no single phrase is wrong but it still reads as AI:

1. **Uniform sentiment.** The emotional temperature never changes. Add a real reaction somewhere (annoyance, surprise, a strong opinion).
2. **No stakes.** Nothing risked, no real failure admitted, no claim that could be wrong. Find the one place to take a position.
3. **Specificity collapse.** Ask "what specifically?" of any sentence. If the answer is "nothing, it just sounds right," it's AI. Push for the real detail (from the user, never invented).
4. **Frictionless smoothness.** No tangents, no second thoughts. A single genuine aside breaks the spell.
