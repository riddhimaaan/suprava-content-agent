# Platform-specific tells: preserve vs strip

For each platform: what to KEEP (the legitimate format), what to KILL (the AI signature), and before/after examples. Read the section that matches the content you are humanizing.

---

## LinkedIn posts

### Keep
- Short paragraphs, often one line each (the "see more" cutoff is real and fine)
- A strong hook line at the top
- White space between thoughts
- A clear point of view
- A genuine ask or takeaway at the end if the user wants one

### Kill
- **"Agree?" / "Thoughts?" / "Am I right?"** as an engagement-bait close. The single most recognizable LinkedIn-AI signature.
- **The fake redemption arc.** "I was broke. I slept in my car. Then I learned one thing. Now I run a 7-figure company." Manufactured hero's journey, almost always fabricated.
- **The dramatic disembodied opener** with no real event behind it. "I got rejected 35 times." If it didn't happen, cut it.
- **Humblebrag wrappers.** "I'm humbled and honored to announce..."
- **The tidy lessons list** where every line is a generic platitude led by → or an emoji.
- **Hashtag stacking.** A block of 10+ generic tags (#Leadership #Growth #Mindset #Success). Keep two or three relevant ones at most, or none.
- **"Here's the thing" / "Here's what most people get wrong"** pivots that frame an ordinary point as a contrarian bombshell.
- All the general tells (negative parallelism, rule of three, em dashes, significance inflation).

## X / Twitter (single tweets and threads)

### Keep
- Lowercase starts, dropped punctuation, abbreviations, slang if it's the user's voice
- A sharp hook
- Thread structure and numbering if it's a thread
- Assumed context (X readers don't need everything spelled out)
- A real take, even a spiky one

### Kill
- **Over-cleanliness.** Grammatically immaculate, fully spelled out, every comma correct. On X, polish is the tell. Loosen it.
- **The thread-bait template.** "I studied 100 X. Here's what I learned: 🧵" followed by evenly tidy numbered lessons.
- **Negative parallelism as a "banger."** "It's not about working harder. It's about working on the right things." This is the default AI tweet shape. Break it.
- **The perfectly balanced two-part tweet with an em dash.** "You don't need more time — you need fewer priorities."
- **The colon-then-clean-list setup.** "Most people get this wrong. Here's the truth:" then a symmetrical list.
- **Motivational-poster voice.** Quotable, weightless, true of everyone and no one.

## YouTube scripts (long-form and Shorts)

The human target here is **spoken language**, which is different from written prose. People don't talk in balanced sentences.

### Keep
- Bracketed directions and cues if present ([B-roll], [cut to demo], [pause], on-screen text notes)
- The hook in the first few seconds
- Direct address to the viewer ("you")
- Structure that fits the video's length
- A real CTA if the user wants one (just not the robotic placement)

### Kill
- **"In this video, I'm going to show you exactly how to..."** The throat-clearing promise-stack intro. Start with the actual hook instead.
- **"But before we get into it, smash that like and subscribe."** The formula-placed CTA dropped where every template says to put it.
- **"Now, you might be wondering..."** AI invents an imagined viewer question to bridge sections. Nobody was wondering. Just make the transition.
- **"So as we discussed..." / "To recap..." / "Remember, the key point is..."** Runtime padding by restatement.
- **Flat narrator cadence.** "First, let's talk about X. Next, we'll look at Y. Finally, Z." Section after section of equal weight, no story, no digression.
- **The hollow motivational outro.** "So there you have it. Now you have everything you need. The only thing between you and success is action. So go make it happen."
- **Written-prose tells leaking into speech:** no contractions, semicolons, "moreover," "furthermore."

### Make it spoken
- Use contractions everywhere (you're, it's, don't, here's).
- Vary line length hard. A three-word punch. Then a longer sentence that wanders a little the way people actually talk when they're explaining something they care about.
- Let one real aside in ("and yeah, I learned this the hard way").
- Read it out loud. If you stumble or run out of breath, the sentence is too long or too written.

## Instagram captions and Reels

### Keep
- A conversational, personal tone
- One clear idea
- A light emoji or two if it matches the user's voice
- A simple CTA if wanted ("save this," "send to a friend") used once, naturally

### Kill
- **Emoji-as-bullet-system.** Every line led by a themed emoji from the same family. One human touch is fine; a system is a tell.
- **The CTA stack.** "Save this 📌 Follow for more 👀 Share with a friend 🔁 Comment below 👇" all piled at the end.
- **The disembodied caps hook** with no real moment behind it.
- **Reel scripts with the same flat narrator cadence** as AI YouTube, just shorter.

## Cold email and outreach

These tells get prospects to delete on sight, so they directly cost reply rate.

### Keep
- Short. Under ~80 words is ideal.
- A subject line of about 4-7 words that sounds like a human sent it
- One specific, real detail about the recipient (if the user supplied it)
- One clear, low-friction ask
- The user's actual signature/intro (moved to the bottom, not the opening line)

### Kill
- **"I hope this email finds you well."** Instant delete. Cut it.
- **"I came across your profile / company and was impressed."** Fake-specific. Signals a mail-merge with no research.
- **"My name is X and I'm from Y, a leading provider of..."** Nobody cares who you are in line one. Lead with them.
- **Visible merge seams.** "Hey {FirstName}, love what you're doing at {Company}!" applies to anyone.
- **Feature-dump rule of three.** "save time, increase revenue, and streamline operations." Vague trio, no outcome, no number.
- **"I'd love to pick your brain / hop on a quick 15-minute call."** Templated soft ask.
- **"I wanted to reach out because..."** Throat-clearing. Say the thing.
- **Flawless three-paragraph symmetry.** Real busy people fire off slightly messy notes. Perfect structure reads generated.
- **"Just circling back / bumping this to the top of your inbox."** Templated follow-up that screams automated sequence.

---

## Cross-platform truth

The fastest single move to humanize anything: replace one generic claim with one real, specific, checkable detail the user supplied. A number, a name, a date, a real opinion. That does more than removing fifty em dashes. And never invent that detail.
